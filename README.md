# Murali Swayambu

### Independent Researcher & Author

I am an independent researcher and author studying structural risks at the intersection of **human cognition, machine speed, behavioural risk, and digital decision systems**.

My work focuses particularly on **edge-decision environments** — situations where time pressure, information density, emotional vulnerability, and machine-speed systems can affect human decision-making.

My approach is **architectural rather than algorithmic**. I examine how system design may create, amplify, or reduce risks at the point where human decisions meet digital systems.

---

## Research Focus

My current research explores:

* Human–Machine Decision Systems
* Behavioural Risk
* Cognitive Pressure
* Temporal Stability
* Information Density
* Digital Fraud Environments
* Digital Engagement Systems
* Human–Machine Decision Chains
* Zero-PII Architecture
* Decision-making at the edge of irreversible action

The frameworks and propositions are exploratory unless specifically identified otherwise. They are offered for **scrutiny, collaboration, replication, falsification, and improvement**.

---

## Research Method

My method combines literature study, systems analysis, prototype development, structured self-observation, technological and market analysis, and critical discussion with colleagues and readers.

I use multiple AI language models as structured critique instruments to test internal consistency, research boundary compliance, extraction of alternative and nearby interpretations, and improve documentation.

**AI-assisted review does not replace empirical validation, specialist judgement, or formal peer review.**

## Empirical Research

A central objective of this research programme is to move from conceptual frameworks and prototype instruments toward **empirical examination**.

Areas of interest include:

* Controlled behavioural testing
* Human–machine decision-chain experiments
* Cognitive-pressure testing
* Information-density experiments
* Temporal-pressure measurements
* Prototype evaluation
* Replication
* Falsification
* Statistical analysis
* Independent examination of proposed mechanisms

The objective is not to confirm a framework in advance.

**The evidence should determine what survives testing.**
