## About

I study how people make decisions under time pressure and heavy information load — particularly at the **edge** between two kinds of decisions:

- **Irreversible decisions** — once acted on, they cannot be undone.
- **Reversible decisions with cost** — technically undoable, but they leave a residual impact (financial, relational, or emotional) that carries forward into the next decision.

I look at this pattern in two connected settings: **human behaviour** (addiction, relationships, trading, everyday choices under pressure) and **human–machine interaction** (how AI-assisted or high-speed digital systems affect a person's ability to pause and reconsider before acting).

My background is not in academia. I hold a **B.Sc. in Statistics (University of Madras, 1992–1996)** and spent about 25 years in payroll systems and operational work, followed by a period in gig-economy delivery work — which is where I began observing these dynamics directly.

My method combines literature review, systems analysis, prototype tools, and structured self-observation. I use AI language models as drafting/critique aids, not as a substitute for empirical testing or formal peer review.
